Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde (1941)

Victor Fleming
113 minutes
Horror, Science Fiction

English
Rating:
3.5 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde concerns itself literally and directly with the nature of evil and its occupation of space in the human psyche.

The performances are good; Spencer Tracy plays a very convincing Jekyll and a deeply creepy and unsettling Hyde, making his dual performance one of particular note. Ingrid Bergman initially plays her character as giddy, game, and a bit goofy much to the fixation of Jekyll, Hyde, and the audience. This is contrasted with her sheer terror later as Hyde torments her however he can.

The make-up work involved in turning the respectable Jekyll into the insane Hyde is not overdone and is more impressive for it. The sequences marking the characters' transformation are sufficiently strange; the film achieved a visual effect which was difficult at the time of overlaying images to create a visual progression, in this case to and from Jekyll and Hyde. The result is surprisingly convincing and surreal. But other surreal imagery stands out, such as when our two female leads appear in the place of horses being whipped or when their images are imposed over bottles of wine being uncorked.

The film makes a convincing argument that decoupling good and evil destroys the human conscience, though Jekyll's "evil" sometimes amounts more to juvenile naughtiness. Still, the message we are given at the end is powerful, as Jekyll can only chemically, temporarily, stave off the possession of Hyde.

As it turns out, good and evil seem to have an inextricable relationship; to attempt to unbind them is courting doom, as your evil side does not go away; rather it manifests itself more fiercely and independently. And now, with the evil side totally detached from the good, the moral compass that only wiggled before now begins to spin.

Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde is a fun examination of the nature of good and evil, propped up by solid performances and impressive visual effects and make-up. This is one transformation horror fans won’t want to miss.
