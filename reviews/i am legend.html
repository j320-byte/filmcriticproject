I Am Legend (2007)

Francis Lawrence
101 minutes
Action, Horror

English
Rating:
3.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

I Am Legend mostly succeeds as a piece of post-apocalyptic fiction but has some unevenness in quality and a complicated relationship with its source. Still, the film is in possession of story virtues.

It goes beyond the action and spends significant time examining the psyche of our sole survivor. His situation and perspective are similarly bleak, only made worse as the state of the world wears away at his means of survival.

Robert's life in fallen New York is interesting as we see him hunting, trapping infected, and running trials to try to recreate his immunity.

The film has a strong emotional core organized around its protagonist. His backstory, though not the central narrative, establishes him as a few things: a member of the military, a husband, a father, and someone willing to do anything for his family. And, of course, he loves his dog.

The monsters suffer from an overreliance on CGI to create their look. This is a particular weakness, because the monsters have retained many human characteristics; make-up and clever cinematography may have been used, but should have been the visual center of the monsters.

The film offers an interesting zombie-approximate narrative. But it's undermined by the fact that its source contains a more distinct and darker vision of the apocalypse. What we see as mindless monsters in the film are much closer to vampires in the book, requiring a stake through the heart and possessing intelligence. In fact, without the source's vision, the title of the film becomes a bit meaningless.

An entertaining if incomplete experience, I Am Legend will satisfy genre fans. Those looking for source fidelity need not apply.
