In Bruges (2008)

Martin McDonagh
107 minutes
Comedy, Crime, Drama

English
Rating:
4.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

The first thing to stand out about In Bruges is the pensive piano music set against somber imagery of the apparent purgatory our protagonists find themselves in. Each serving a different sentence, the two make for a compelling duo.

The core of this film's strengths lies with tight writing and comedic performances which never fail to land.

Colin Farrell's comedy is relentless. His character, Ray, declares shortly after arriving at their destination, "If I had grown up on a farm and was retarded, maybe Bruges would impress me." Later, while on cocaine, Ray argues with a bigoted dwarf about an impending race war, asking which side various groups of people would fight for, much to the dwarf’s exasperation. Colin Farrell nails the juvenile nature of his character perfectly.

Brendan Gleeson acts as both pseudo-parent to Farrell as well as weathered hitman. His dynamic with Ralph Fiennes is similarly multifaceted, with Gleeson acting as dutiful soldier, rebel, and defender. Gleeson's character is the moral heart of the narrative, our much-needed counterweight to the ever-present comedy.

Later in the film, Ralph Fiennes delivers an explosively funny performance complete with excessive profanity and physical expressiveness to match. His character, though mostly just screaming nonsense, states his view of people without restraint: "you're an inanimate fucking object!"

The film captures the dreariness of Bruges without creating a boring or suffocating atmosphere. Of particular aid is the fascinating visual profile Bruges offers, with its old architecture, ornate trappings, and many, many alcoves.

Some may see Bruges as purgatory, but Ray speculates it may actually be what Hell is composed of; a never-ending Bruges. From that standpoint, the film can be seen as chronicling Ray's damnation in the face of his sins. But other characters are held to account with more certainty than Ray, leaving us questioning his ultimate fate.

Completely inappropriate, founded on great performances, and always hilarious (especially when it shouldn’t be) In Bruges is a more than worthwhile entry by Martin McDonagh.
