Infinitely Polar Bear (2014)

Maya Forbes
88 minutes
Comedy, Drama

English
Rating:
3.5 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

Infinitely Polar Bear presents a tumultuously authentic, autobiographical look into the life of the director, Maya Forbes, and her experience as the child of a bipolar patient.

The performances are solid all around. Mark Ruffalo plays a hilariously unhinged, but distinctly troubled man, Cam, with efficacy and bombasticism. Like many unmedicated bipolar people he is prone to flights of fancy, massive outbursts, and general psychosocial instability. Zoe Saldaña, as Cam’s wife Maggie, strikes an incredibly complex balance between deep care for her husband and a desire for reform on his part.

Imogene Wolodarsky, the elder of the two daughters, is given a charming scene when she sneaks over to Cam’s halfway house and demands to know what his long-term plans are. She and Ashley Aufderheide as the younger sister excel in the range of their reactions to their father’s condition. Their characters’ developing understanding of Cam’s situation is implied by the younger daughter saying he is “polar bear” as a description.

The depiction of bipolar disorder was not only accurate, but compassionate, and offered humor that is rooted in truth. It’s okay to laugh at the ridiculous situations Cam’s condition creates; but there is still a requirement to address what is causing those ridiculous situations. Cam comes to terms with this when he comes home, after another reckless encounter, and takes his lithium, which he is normally very resistant to.

The material being autobiographical is a great strength in many ways but a liability in others. The progression of the story sometimes seems unclear with some big moments feeling undersold, but such moments remain exceptions. Perhaps the weakest part of the film, the ending was decidedly uncertain; it was not negative or ambiguous, but was very abrupt.

Committed to honesty and compassion, Infinitely Polar Bear is an insightful look into those who live with bipolar disorder; not just the patients, but their profoundly affected families.
