Iron Lung (2026)

Mark Fischbach
125 minutes
Horror, Science Fiction

English
Rating:
3.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

Punching well above its weight both in terms of budget and premise, Iron Lung successfully creates a suffocating environment and an authentically grim world.

The story is thin, staying true to the narrative simplicity of the source material. This is a strength, as it allows a certain amount of latitude in storytelling. The source idea is of course very bizarre but still strong: the universe has gone dark, planets stripped of life, and only a splinter of humanity remains, drifting among the stars. The best part? There are moons covered in oceans of blood. None of it tells a whole story but it’s not meant to: it creates the insane world in which a blood submarine becomes a practical idea and then shoves us inside.

Mark Fischbach’s performance vacillates between hammy and strikingly sincere. His roots as a YouTube star are apparent in his performance, but that is not an altogether bad thing. By the end, Fischbach manages to sell some pretty over-the-top material with great effect.

The practical set of the submarine as well as the blood effects seen throughout the film both serve their purpose well and provoke a visceral response. This is not just seen at the climax, but even earlier in the film when blood can be seen dripping from the walls of the submarine.

The film should be applauded for the amount of screen time featuring only one performer. Such roles are uniquely challenging, both for the performer and for the audience. Iron Lung used the isolation to its advantage, further building upon the already strong oppressive atmosphere.

Iron Lung is ultimately a guarded success, both as a video game adaptation and as evidence of the skills of an incoming filmmaker.
