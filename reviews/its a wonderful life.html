It's a Wonderful Life (1946)

Frank Capra
130 minutes
Drama, Family, Holiday

English
Rating:
5.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

It's a Wonderful Life has a message packed straight into the title, but the greater message is universal: one good person doesn't just have the capacity to change the world for the better; rather, such betterment is inevitable in their presence. The Earth, in deprivation of a single, ordinary man, shifts beneath our feet, out of daylight and into darkness.

The religious worldview of the film ties in well with Christmas, connecting the Holiday season with age-old Christian ideas. Our opening sees angels discussing a grave matter: the disillusionment and suicidality of George Bailey; this sequence is not only visually well executed, but tells an important truth of the film: guardian angels are real and they watch over us even now. We are assured of this by Clarence, who states directly to George his status as George’s guardian angel as well as his assignment. The angels above are not the only striking visual, as it is aided by shots such as the boys sliding haphazardly on the ice, and the sequence depicting the seedy exterior of dark Bedford Falls. These moments are more than visually interesting; they provide indispensable storytelling.

James Stewart's portrayal of George at his lowest is convincing to anyone familiar with despair, mumbling, brooding, and wallowing in his own self-appointed pit of misery; his crisis is legitimate but his assessment of his life is not. He even cynically declares "I'm worth more dead than alive."

It is only after getting a look into the gloom and the dim that George is ready to affirm his self-worth. In a powerful moment, he cries out to God, exclaiming his desire to live.

Sound plays an important role in the beginning and ending, assuring us of one thing: it is not that the bell tolls for no man. It tolls for every man.

With a surprisingly deft message about life and the choice of throwing it away, It's a Wonderful Life remains an incredibly compelling story and an all-time holiday classic.
