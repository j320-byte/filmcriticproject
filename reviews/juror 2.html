Juror #2 (2024)

Clint Eastwood
114 minutes
Crime, Drama, Legal

English
Rating:

★★★★★
★★★★★

Juror #2 examines the corruption of the judicial system by constructing a moral problem with only one available solution: confession.

Recreating the experience of the jury for the viewer, the night of the incident is sometimes depicted with variation, reflecting the witnesses’ different recollections and opinions. The movie does not belabor the courtroom section and, appropriate to the title, focuses on juror deliberation.

One of Tyler’s (Nicholas Hoult) few positive contributions: he helps prevent the first round of voting from resulting in a guilty verdict. This action is, of course, defiled by its primary motivation: self-interest. Spending all of deliberation trying to steer the jury, Tyler systematically looks for a way out of his predicament without accountability. But as he is reminded by his lawyer and AA pal, "We're only as sick as our secrets".

But the problems in this judicial proceeding run much deeper than Tyler. The jury pool is universally tainted: a juror guilty of the crime, a man seeking conviction as a matter of personal vendetta, and a police officer investigating beyond the courtroom. All of the jurors other than Tyler are invested in a speedy verdict to escape their duties. Worsening matters are the unconventional allowances made by the judge, such as visiting the crime scene during deliberation, as well as the prosecutor conducting her own investigation and the unethical guidance of Tyler's lawyer.

Despite the otherwise insoluble nature of his problem, Tyler confesses only twice: for his own sake, to his lawyer, and a second time to prevent alienation from his wife. Tyler sees the truth as currency; he only spends it when he has something to gain. He reinforces his view of the truth later: "sometimes the truth isn't justice".

Tyler's transactional view of truth is fundamentally incompatible with the justice system, as we see him put up a massive struggle to sway it. But a simpler truth is proclaimed elsewhere in the film: "Justice is truth in action".

Feeling deft and fresh despite (or perhaps, because of) Clint Eastwood's seniority, Juror #2 has a compelling premise, which it capitalizes on splendidly.
