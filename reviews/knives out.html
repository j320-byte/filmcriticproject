Knives Out (2019)

Rian Johnson
130 minutes
Mystery

English
Rating:
4.5 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

"Physical evidence can tell a clear story with a forked tongue," our hero assures us early on in Knives Out, and the layered explanation the film provides for our mystery supports this assertion.

The film's greatest strength is its dialogue. Daniel Craig, as Benoit Blanc, delivers his lines with conviction, especially when they are idiomatically peculiar. A good example of this is when he questions Marta as to whether or not she has "a regurgitive reaction to mistruthing."

The scene between Marta and Harlan in the study is a fantastic example of a rapid mood shift in a scene. They are engaged in banter, Harlan accusing the gentle Marta of "elder abuse" for beating him at a game. Shortly after, when the horrifying implications of the medication become clear, we see Ana de Armas convey a quick escalation from complete calm to total panic.

Having left all his wealth and possessions to Marta, Harlan acts as an isolated case of wealth redistribution. Instead of handing it down to his unworthy family and reinforcing everything bad about them, he gave it to someone who was deserving. His spoiled family had only ever taken from him; Marta earned his affection through work and care, and was in much greater need.

The next point is double-edged. Early in the film, Harlan remarks upon the similarities between him and Ransom, living dangerously and being unable to discern a stage prop from a real knife, using the latter to make his point. At the end, in a final attempt at murder, Ransom reaches for a false blade, thinking it is a real one. This stands in contrast with Harlan, who seemed to have enough guile to "tell the difference" between the prop and the weapon. So while Ransom and Harlan may be less alike than Harlan thought, Harlan did correctly predict Ransom's downfall.

The film manages to have a contemporary political conversation which achieves a few things: it feels timely, authentic, and above all lampoons every party involved. This is a noteworthy feat, as political baggage can quickly destroy a film if not handled with care. In this case it served to enrich the comedy.

As weaknesses, the otherwise successful dialogue is accompanied by offerings that are sometimes less successful, or even outright bad, such as the "stupid with two Os" line. As a narrative weakness, the parts of the plot involving Fran felt especially contrived, even if they were a necessary part of our story.

With a fresh and eccentric detective, a star-studded cast, and dialogue that almost always hits the mark, the mystery in Knives Out will leave the viewer saying: "It makes no damn sense... It compels me though."
