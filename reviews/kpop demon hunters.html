KPop Demon Hunters (2025)

Maggie Kang, Chris Appelhans
95 minutes
Animation, Family, Fantasy, Music

English
Rating:
4.5 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

In KPop Demon Hunters, fandom is expressed in a universal language, on offer for all. This turned out to be true for both the story and the film itself, as it greatly exceeded expectations and has broken through many demographic boundaries.

Our heroines are uniquely refreshing, appealing to both modern and age-old ideas about what makes them both distinctly powerful and distinctly feminine. Similarly, despite conflict, female friendship dynamics are depicted very positively.

The characters' visual identities are strong and draw the viewer’s eyes in; this contrasts with many other contemporary animated features which have experimented unsuccessfully with other styles. The choice to make the characters resemble a tried-and-true model, similar to that of Barbie dolls complete with glittery makeup, was far preferable to the present and prevalent predisposition to characters who appear to simply be loosely attached blobs. Other characters’ visual identities stand out as well, such as the holistic doctor with the bulging eyes and the vividly drawn blue tiger.

Alongside the music, the film's greatest strengths are visual. The fight sequences are dynamic, the camera swinging dramatically and precisely as it follows the brawls; use of color is also heightened in these sequences, contrasting the Hunters with their prey. Visual ingenuity is also seen in the variable framerates used to render our characters depending on the situation and scene, an unconventional idea which works incredibly well in execution. Variable framerates have long existed in animated features, especially anime, usually for cost and production reasons; KPop Demon Hunters is unique in turning this into a conscious, stylistic choice.

Shots that stand out as generally powerful are those of the Honmoon tearing in two beneath Rumi's feet and the Saja boys transitioning across multiple large screens to advertise their final performance.

The climax is perfectly primed, allowing the villains one last chance to shine. The sequence that follows is imbued with all the best parts of action anime, princess musicals, and universal messaging. The flourish as Rumi's sword changes is a particularly striking moment.

The film also has a well-developed sense of humor, with the characters taking on heightened cartoonish characteristics in moments of comedy. Funny visuals like the popcorn spewing from the girls’ eyes were also present throughout.

In recent years, animation has undergone significant change in terms of output and audience expectation; a quality ceiling has been fixed above many of these films. This movie bursts through that ceiling and keeps going. Energetic as it is infectious, KPop Demon Hunters won’t be taken off the playlist anytime soon.
