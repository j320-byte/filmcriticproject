KPop Demon Hunters (2025)

Maggie Kang, Chris Appelhans
95 minutes
Animation, Family, Fantasy, Music

English
Rating:
4.5 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

In KPop Demon Hunters, fandom is expressed in a universal language, always on offer and available to all.

This turned out to be true for both the story and the film itself, as it greatly exceeded expectations and broke through demographic barriers. Shortly after release, the stardom of its characters had become a reality and was no longer just an imagined part of the film's world.

This, as well as everything else about KPop Demon Hunters, acts as an invitation into that world.

Our heroines are uniquely refreshing, appealing to both modern and age-old ideas about what makes them powerful, feminine, and unique. The friendship amongst our main trio is depicted very positively, with a heavy focus put on honesty and trust.

The characters’ visual identities are eye-catching and super-beautified. While other studios have experimented with the blob-character school of design, Sony said "to hell with that" and delivered characters with varied stature, angular designs, and cartoonized counterparts. The Hunters may as well be warrior Barbies, complete with glamorous makeup, weaponry, and intense style. Even minor characters avoid the trap of uniformity. Highlights include the doctor with the bulging eyes, the crying hunks, and the excited grandmas.

The film's visual strengths go beyond character design. The fight sequences are dynamic, the camera swinging dramatically and precisely as it follows the brawls; use of color is also heightened in these sequences, contrasting the Hunters with their prey. Variable framerate plays an important role too, rendering our characters differently depending on whether they are in combat or otherwise experiencing a moment of high animation.

This film refused to cosplay its way out of the musical element. The soundtrack is fairly superb, with multiple standouts. The beat in "How It's Done" is perfect for the combat introduction of our characters. "Golden" could have been released independently from the movie and it would have still been fantastic. "What It Sounds Like" starts in quiet simplicity and amps up to stadium-level-sonic intensity. This further validates our characters; these KPop stars have actual music attached to them, not just an empty story.

The climax is perfectly primed, allowing the villains one last chance to shine with the song "Your Idol" featuring dramatic choral vocals with contemporary pop. The stage is set for the final confrontation. The sequence that follows is imbued with all the best parts of action anime, princess musicals, and universal messaging. The Hunters must fight their way across the distance separating them and it is only with their most powerful weapon, music which inspires people, that they are able to prevail.

Many of KPop Demon Hunters’ peers have suffered due to industry-level changes in style and storytelling sensibilities; consequently, a quality ceiling has been fixed above animation films. This movie bursts through that ceiling and keeps going. As energetic as it is infectious, KPop Demon Hunters won’t be taken off the playlist anytime soon.
