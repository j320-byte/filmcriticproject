Night of the Living Dead (1968)

George A. Romero
96 minutes
Exploitation, Horror

English
Rating:
5.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

The seminal work in zombie fiction, Night of the Living Dead, peels back the horror of its situation through violence and newscasts. "All persons who die during this crisis from whatever cause will come back to life to seek human victims," we are assured by a newsman in the film, cementing for decades the prevailing pop culture definition of a "zombie".

Made on a shoestring budget and worked on tirelessly by a devoted crew, this film acts as an exercise in simplicity in filmmaking.

The set was a home slated for demolition which lacked running water. Still, the filmmakers stayed at the farmhouse, using a nearby creek to bathe and as a source of water. A funny anecdote from the filmmakers includes a memory of filling sheep intestines with water to give them substance. Even the false blood was cheaply produced, alternating between the use of red ink and chocolate syrup (as the film was black and white and would not register the true colors).

The zombies were brought to life through make-up and performance, with many extras committing to their minor roles. This can be seen in the fervor with which the zombies fight one another for food, or take hard falls as they are dispatched. The quality of their performance can be summed up best by the police chief: "Yeah they're dead. They're all messed up."

The film comes packaged with commentary about conformity and man as its own worst enemy. The zombies are described as "ordinary-looking people, misshapen monsters, people who look like they're in a trance, and creatures that look like people but behave like animals." This makes the relationship between man and zombie clear: they're us.

Man as his own worst enemy is explored through the conflict in the house. Our characters spend valuable time arguing when they should be focused on survival and highly incentivized toward amicable solutions. Still, Harry cannot stand to be without power, and Ben is completely unwilling to waver from his plan.

The Night of the Living Dead is a uniquely bleak film. Ben survives the night, rising from the basement just as the lawmen are arriving and killing the zombies. Seen from afar and treated as a zombie, our lead character receives only this as his eulogy: "Good shot! OK, he's dead; let's go get 'im. That's another one for the fire."

To any zombie fan who fails to pay respect to Night of the Living Dead, the reviewer has only one thing to say to you: "I oughta drag you out there and feed you to those things!"
