Night of the Living Dead (1990)

Tom Savini
92 minutes
Exploitation, Horror

English
Rating:
1.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

Night of the Living Dead (1990), remaking the infamous original of the same name, seemed poised for success. Romero, director of the original and grandfather of the genre, convinced practical effects wizard Tom Savini to direct the film. The producers, John A. Russo and Russ Streiner, were associated with the original production. With the involvement of so many of the people related to the previous productions, it seemed impossible to fail.

But fail, they did.

Night of the Living Dead (1990) experienced a troubled production, with producer interference complicating Tom Savini's first feature directorial effort.

The film immediately establishes the trademark qualities of its performances: hokey, over-the-top, and delivered with the timing of an epileptic drummer. This is unfortunate, as some of the performers, particularly Tony Todd, perform well in other films; here they are limited by both script and direction.

The movie makes obvious alterations to events within the house, such as the mishap with the TV. Still, some story beats hew too closely to the original for their own good, creating a "been there, done that" effect. Some changes, however, had potential. In the original, Barbara was near catatonic; in this film she shows herself to be adaptable. She even successfully identifies the way out of their situation, though none of the group takes heed of her advice. It may be an overstatement to call this an improvement on the character, but it is certainly a compelling vision of Barbara.

Completely confoundingly, the blood and gore effects of the film fall flat. How this is possible with Savini at the helm is unclear; his past work speaks for itself. None of the violence lands.

Night of the Living Dead (1990) is a needless iteration on a timeless classic which manages only to pick at the bones of the original.
