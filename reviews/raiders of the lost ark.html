Raiders of the Lost Ark (1981)

Steven Spielberg
115 minutes
Action, Adventure

English
Rating:
5.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

Through clever use of religious iconography and a historical setting, Raiders of the Lost Ark masterfully authenticates its world and the characters in it.

From the moment the film starts, the audience finds themselves transported to a remote region of Peru with a rugged adventurer who, fearless of death, plunders a lost ancient temple. Even after the opening sequence, there is a distinct lived-in quality to the world. The viewer gets the unmistakable sense that, despite being the first film, this is far from the first adventure. The movie exists as if it stands upon the shoulders of a much larger body of stories.

Harrison Ford is Indiana Jones, without dispute. He conveys surly frustration, heavy charm, and deep intellectualism as interchangeable parts of the character, thus bringing Indiana to life. Indy's charm is especially apparent when on screen with his romantic opposite, Marion, played by Karen Allen. Alternating between fighting and tenderness, the two characters possess a dynamic of frustrated but intense attraction.

The film bootstraps its premise through a familiar time period supplemented by recollections from Sunday School. For those without such recollections, Indiana brought an absurdly large Bible, complete with illustrations, with which to make his point. The Ark is a holy relic of incalculable power and one that Hitler has set his eyes upon. This is a grave error, as later in the film we see Indiana explicitly and loudly instruct Marion not to gaze upon the open Ark when its awesome power is on display.

Visual effects do their part in selling the film's mythology. The climactic face-melting scene aligns well with our villain's sins and is a stunning display of practical effects. Many directors tried to get in touch with Spielberg regarding union rules on liquefying your performers.

As a globe-trotting film, it was important that each location furthered the feeling of adventure. Every waystation is fantastic and interesting, but the chase is afoot and our characters never stand still for long. From the opening jungle, to the bustling paths of Cairo, and to the final arid island, every setting is perfectly suited to the scene.

With an exceptional story and a daring sense of adventure, this film has generated fans across generations and attained iconic status. Raiders of the Lost Ark contains a deeply immersive world, remains a definitive piece of adventure cinema, and is an absolute must-watch.
