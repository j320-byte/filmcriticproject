Silverado (1985)

Lawrence Kasdan
133 minutes
Western

English
Rating:
5.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

Silverado is not only star-studded, excellently performed and written, and a fantastic western; it is also much damn funnier than it has any right to be.

Despite not being billed as such, the comedy in the film is continuous. The film establishes this early, when Kevin Kline is asked his ultimate destination and, still in his underwear after being robbed, nervously looks at the horse and asks, "Where's the pinto going?". Another similarly funny line is delivered by Danny Glover, as he forcefully declares, "Now I don't want to kill you and you don't want to be dead."

Kevin Kline, Scott Glenn, Kevin Costner, Brian Dennehy, Danny Glover, Linda Hunt; the stellar performances are simply too numerous to list. Even the minor performances, such as Jeff Fahey as Tyree, are deeply and effectively characterized. Scott Glenn plays the part of stoic older brother well, driven chiefly by family. Danny Glover conveys his character's plight expertly, a man seeking justice for his father, who is being driven from his own land. Even the lone island of Paden, played by Kevin Kline, spends his days searching the horizon for familial figures, which he finds in individuals like Stella, played by Linda Hunt. The value of family and camaraderie over power and that which is material is present throughout.

Silverado is situated during a small resurgence of Western films in the 1980s. But this affords certain visual strengths that older western films simply couldn’t recreate. The color is striking and captures the landscapes like colors are painted on a canvas. Props, such as our firearms, our horse tackle, and even the liquor bottles, all receive a high attention to detail that would have been lost on inferior formats.

Watching this film feels like going on a long but wonderful journey in a wide and lived-in world. After viewing, you may find you have to clap the dust off your shoes. When watching Silverado, we can only relate to Paden and Stella midway through the film as they toast: "Here's to the good stuff."
