Spider-Man (2002)

Sam Raimi
121 minutes
Action, Superhero

English
Rating:
4.5 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

Spider-Man represents an expertly coordinated collision between comic books and film. The visuals show a world ripped straight from the page: there is a fairly high level of saturation and shots are often framed like panels in a comic. Spider-Man's suit is incredibly designed and modernized, with the occasionally reflective eye lenses standing out vividly as they capture the cityscape beneath him.

The most important characters are all brought to life from the page, with particular note going to Cliff Robertson, who is convincing both in his performance as a loving uncle as well as a dying man; Uncle Ben is an inseparable part of the Spider-Man mythos, and Cliff Robertson’s gentleness perfectly suited the character. On the other hand, Willem Dafoe is convincingly hammy as the Green Goblin, with a tongue almost as sharp as Spider-Man's; his characterization closely aligns with the unhinged source character. The sequence of him talking to himself in the mirror is chilling.

The web sounds come with a satisfying thwip, giving sonic identity to our hero’s powers that the comics never could. This is a phenomenon seen throughout the movie: an imbuing of visual and audible characteristics that are unavailable to print media. Another good example of this is the fight sequences; previously broken up into a small set of images, Spider-Man and the Goblin can be seen trading blows in real-time.

All these components come together to create a movie that feels a hell of a lot like a comic book. With color and character to spare, Spider-Man represents the successful launch point of a great trilogy.
