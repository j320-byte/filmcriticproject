The NeverEnding Story (1984)

Wolfgang Petersen
102 minutes
Family, Fantasy

English
Rating:
4.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

Staring at the death of imagination straight in its ugly, inky eyes, The NeverEnding Story is a tale of creativity and hope.

The film's primary antagonistic power, the Nothing, is an all-engulfing force eating away at reality; it stems from the loss of imagination. This is an unusually philosophically dense antagonistic force for a children's movie, provoking questions of existentialism and morality.

The protagonist, Bastian, is the perfect vessel for escapism; he lives in his own personally defined fantasy world that only he understands. His father is not only dismissive of Bastian's "head in the clouds" nature; he is also seen minimizing the grief Bastian is experiencing after the death of his mother.

Morla is another philosophical anchor point of the film, a large tortoise who is very wise but has grown cynical and jaded with age. Morla even expresses mild excitement at dying, as it would at least break up the monotony of things. Worldviews like Morla's create the Nothing.

One conception of the film sees the Nothing as being the massive force of Bastian's grief, threatening to destroy everything. Similarly, the stalking Gmork can be seen as those seeking to enforce conformity on Bastian. As Fantasia is torn to pieces, there remains only one solution: an act of imagination. Bastian is asked to name the Empress of Fantasia, and in doing so sparks imagination and restores Fantasia.

The practical effects, makeup, and puppetry present throughout the film are impressive. The rock giant, Morla, and the Gmork stand out as highly effective.

Tonally, the movie is a bit strange for one aimed at children. The scene of Artax's demise is particularly traumatizing. Falcor, meaningful or not, is depicted as strangely creepy. His voice and face are a bit too excited as he requests physical favors from Atreyu. It is treated more like the petting of a dog, but Falcor's sentience alters the dynamic significantly.

The film's dialogue and visuals create an almost surreal element, leading the reviewer to question what substances may have been involved in the production of this film.

Visually unique and generally unconventional, The NeverEnding Story is mostly rewarded for its experimentation with metafiction and the fantasy genre in general.
