The Substance (2024)

Coralie Fargeat
141 minutes
Horror

English
Rating:
5.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

Visceral and thought-provoking, The Substance comes with a thematic message delivered almost entirely through incisively effective visuals and with stunning clarity.

Using the horror genre to explore its themes, the film delves into the blood and guts of self-image, self-division, and the relationship they share with unrealistic beauty standards. Of key interest is the journey women take as they age and observe that the expectations put upon them remain the same. The opening succinctly conveys Elizabeth’s story before we meet her properly through an economic shot which chronicles the weathering of her star on the walk of fame.

The theme of division of self is made literal, as the protagonist uses a black-market drug so she may share two bodies in order to become young again. As they are reliant on one another, an unsteady relationship forms between these two sides of the same person. This mirrors the event of internalizing beauty standards and becoming two selves: the one that is presentable and then the true, aging self. The aging self must perform all the labor necessary for the presentable self to exist. This plays out as Elizabeth and her younger self, Sue, quarrel over how to coexist.

This is interesting, because the source of the black-market drug has consistently reminded Elizabeth that she and Sue are not separate people. But the adherence to beauty dogma creates an internal worldview of “how they see me” versus “how I see me”.

The ultimate result is a self-adversarial relationship, seen in Elizabeth struggling to unify herself as the two different versions of her compete. In one of the most startling sequences of the film, both bodies gain consciousness and engage in a violent and kinetically shot fight. Sue kills Elizabeth; beauty won and the old self has been subsumed by something else. Elizabeth has been told to "respect the balance" but there never was any balance; the relationship between these two selves was always asymmetric.

Practical effects are center stage in this film, which will delight many horror fans. The suit used for Monstro Elizasue is an exercise in the grotesque, a fitting comment on the end result of the dogma to beauty. Almost as if to add to the commentary Margaret Qualley wore prosthetics to "enhance" her body; surely attracting the attention of men, this itself makes the point that men are titillated by beauty standards which are not just unrealistic, but unreal altogether. As for the blood, someone, somewhere, is missing a lot of red food coloring. It appears Coralie Fargeat chose to use it in one of the bloodiest sequences horror has ever seen. This further reinforces the idea that the entire industry is culpable, as they are all stained in blood.

Seek beauty relentlessly at your own peril; for when you find that beautiful self, the question will eventually arise: "Has she started yet? Eating away at you?"
