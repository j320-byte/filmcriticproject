Thoroughbreds (2018)

Cory Finley
92 minutes
Comedy, Thriller

English
Rating:
4.0 / 5.0
★★★★★
★★★★★

Thoroughbreds lays out the mirrored facets of our characters’ personalities and orchestrates a rearrangement of them; it reorganizes something old, creates something new, or perhaps even accomplishes both.

When a person experiences a huge swing in their identity, was this due to an actual fundamental change, or a simple rearrangement of their existing internal architecture? This question is close to the film's thematic center of gravity.

Symmetrical and well centered shots are present throughout, a representation of the shifting dichotomous nature of our lead duo. Lily, portrayed with great dynamism by Anya Taylor-Joy, starts as deeply emotional, but remains highly repressed. In Olivia Cooke's similarly self-altering performance, we see Amanda, a self-proclaimed emotionless person who, in her apparent anhedonia, spends her days in mimicry and muted musing.

The film also asks the moral question: without proper human feelings, what capacity does the person have for moral compliance? Or even basic moral understanding? Amanda has her own philosophy: "[It] doesn't necessarily make me a bad person, it just means I have to work a little harder than everyone else to be good".

The film's presentation of the above exploration is aided by an excellent atmosphere created through understated, but still crystal-clear, color. Sound also contributes significantly to atmosphere, the music being in possession of an unsettling and lurking quality; this also effectively creates tension, as it constantly cues the viewer to some impending dark development. While dialogue dominates, both in time and quality, thoughtful and wordless sequences are present too, such as when Amanda walks through Lily's house and surmises details of her lifestyle.

The foundational question of Thoroughbreds remains: do we change or simply rearrange? It doesn't seek to answer this question, instead calling attention to the alterations in our characters; it seems that they may have exaggerated certain characteristics that before were repressed. This is evident in Amanda's uncharacteristically sentimental letter and Lily's comment about its contents: "I don't know. I just threw it away."

Carefully crafted dialogue, well tuned tension, and the electric dynamic between the two leads imbue Thoroughbreds with complete command of the audience's attention.
